\title{SynAP-Fib: Synergistic Anatomical Priors for Fine-Grained Ultrasound
Liver Fibrosis Grading in Schistosomiasis}

\author{Ziyang~Xu, \textit{et al.}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Ultrasound grading of schistosomiasis-associated liver fibrosis is
operator-dependent and anatomically structured: images are acquired under a
standardized six-position protocol, and fibrotic change is annotated with
coarse bounding boxes. We present SynAP-Fib, a fine-grained grading model that
augments an ordinal grading backbone with two anatomical prior branches: a
six-class position head and a weak-box lesion attention head. Each branch
enters grading through a gated residual injection with a bidirectionally
detached gradient boundary, so anatomical supervision shapes the branches
while the grading pathway consumes only their predicted outputs, requiring no
anatomical metadata at inference. On a multicenter cohort of 108,709
region-of-interest images from 6,373 patients across 35 centers, a $2\times2$
comparison on the patient-held-out test set showed that introducing either
prior alone degraded the prespecified composite clinical risk, whereas the
joint configuration retained baseline-level grading accuracy, produced the
best observed composite risk of the four configurations, and emitted
anatomical position and weak lesion localization outputs at a 5.9\% parameter
and 2.8\% arithmetic overhead. The joint configuration and the baseline were
not distinguishable on the primary endpoint under a paired bootstrap analysis,
so the joint configuration is described as best observed rather than as an
established improvement.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Liver fibrosis, ultrasound, ordinal grading, anatomical priors, auxiliary
supervision, schistosomiasis.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section*{Data availability}
The full clinical ultrasound image dataset analysed in this study is not
publicly available because it contains patient-derived medical images and
associated clinical information collected under institutional ethics approval
and is subject to participant privacy and data-protection requirements.
De-identified data necessary to verify the main findings are available under
restricted access for non-commercial academic research, subject to
institutional approval, applicable ethics requirements, and execution of a
data-use agreement. Requests should be directed to the corresponding authors.
