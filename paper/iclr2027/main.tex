\begin{abstract}
Medical images often contain structured information that is available during training but unreliable at deployment. We investigate whether such information can be encoded as explicit, anatomically grounded concepts and used as an interpretable pathway for fine-grained prediction. We present \method, an enhanced ResNet-50 model for schistosomiasis-associated liver-fibrosis grading. The model predicts two intermediate concepts: a posterior distribution over six anatomical positions and a weakly supervised lesion weak-box map. A gated residual mechanism with a bidirectionally detached gradient boundary injects these predicted concept representations into the grading pathway. At inference, the model relies solely on image-derived concepts and requires neither position metadata nor lesion bounding boxes. On an independent patient-held-out test set containing 4,107 images from 240 patients, a prespecified comparison of five configurations shows that the joint concept pathway achieves the best observed composite clinical risk while preserving baseline-level grading performance. The joint model also produces anatomical-position and weak-box localization outputs, providing clinically relevant anatomical and lesion signals, with only 5.9\% additional parameters and 2.8\% additional arithmetic cost. These results establish an efficient framework for integrating anatomically grounded concepts into fine-grained medical-image prediction and provide a concrete foundation for future concept-intervention studies and clinically oriented model interpretation.\end{abstract}

\section{Limitations and Broader Impact}
The study concerns schistosomiasis-associated fibrosis and a patient-held-out split whose four test centers are also represented in training. It therefore does not establish unseen-center generalization or applicability to other etiologies. The weak-box target is not pixel-level lesion ground truth. The model has been evaluated with one ResNet-50 backbone and one seed. These boundaries are part of the intended interpretation. The system is designed to expose additional image-derived signals to a clinician; it should not be used as an autonomous diagnostic device without prospective validation and appropriate clinical oversight.
